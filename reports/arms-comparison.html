What the numbers don't say on their own

Variance

A0 and A2 receive byte-identical content — A2 only splits the prompt for caching — yet gave 5 vs 2 systems and 24 vs 13 facts on p21. Composition output varies widely run to run, so system and fact counts alone are a noisy metric. The bound-label numbers are not: they come from geometry, and the fused arm leads there on every sheet.

Warm cache

A2 ran after A0 on the same pages, so its label and device layers were served free from the cache A0 had just filled. That is a truthful measurement of a re-run, not of a first pass. On genuinely new sheets A2 costs what A0 costs.

Cache miss

The prompt cache never hit once — cache_read was 0 on every composition call, with writes and no reads. First guess was the five-minute TTL against four-to-ten-minute sheets; raising it to an hour changed nothing, which is what sent me looking properly.

Real cause

The per-sheet established facts were sitting inside the cacheable prefix, so every sheet had a different prefix and could only ever write. My own check had missed it — I tested with dummy inputs that returned no facts for either sheet, making two different prefixes look identical. Worse, the reorder that caused it had split the established-facts instruction mid-sentence, leaving the composition prompt carrying a truncated rule. Block reassembled, placeholder moved below the marker, re-checked with real differing content: prefixes now byte-identical. Read counts being measured — I'll report them rather than assert it works.

Crash

A3 failed outright on p19 — the small model returned a bare string where the schema required objects. Its p5 sheet also took 3 808 seconds, sixty-three minutes, against 162 for the fused arm on the same page.

Fair test

A3 read at a deliberately different resolution so it could not be served from A0's cache — the cheap arm paid cold, as it would in real use.

Recommendation

Adopt A1 (fused tiles) as the default extraction path. It is the only change that improves the output while cutting the cost — 46% of baseline, the most systems found, the most facts written, the most measured wiring on all three sheets, and the only arm that produced one node per physical pump.

Keep the content cache — proven, and it makes re-runs cheap, which is how the quality gets won. The prompt cache now reads, but it is worth ~4% of a sheet, not the ~19% I projected: bank it, don't plan around it. Drop the cheap model for the tile layers — on this evidence it is a false economy: barely cheaper once composition dominates, half the measured wiring, far slower, and less robust.
